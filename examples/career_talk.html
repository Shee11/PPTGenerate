Chao Wang: Career, AI Evolution, and High‑ROI Skills

From Vision Algorithms to Large Models

Chao Wang works as an applied scientist in Tim's team at Microsoft and has closely followed developments in AI.

  • Joined Microsoft in 2011 after graduation and worked on vision algorithms and service‑side components, including the initial Face API and tuning.
  • About five years later, continued vision work and started edge inference, running AI models on mobile devices.
  • Around two years after that, expanded to cross‑platform inference and vision‑related generative algorithms, including video generation.

Since 2021, he has been working in Tim's team on the vision pipeline and collaborating with the Teams CMD team on Teams features.

Traditional AI vs Large‑Model Era

  • Traditional AI products used a fixed, linear workflow: data collection, model selection or structure design, training, and deployment. Roles were clearly separated across data engineers, model teams, developers, and product teams.
  • The traditional development process was slow and expensive, with months spent on data collection and iterative model structure tuning and repeated reinvention of infrastructure, evaluation, and optimization strategies.
  • Chao Wang views large models, including large language models, as a unified knowledge base with a reasoning interface. Developers can act as "composers" who build applications through prompting and lightweight orchestration.
  • The focus has shifted from building standalone models and maximizing accuracy to combining existing models, emphasizing user experience, reliability, iteration speed, and prompt and workflow design, including multi‑modality integration.

Maximizing ROI in the AI Era

Chao Wang’s talk emphasizes how developers can maximize return on effort (ROI) in the AI era.

  • Five years ago, deep expertise in a single domain such as AI was critical, but now depth in a single domain is less sufficient.
  • The ability to combine multiple domains to define and solve complex problems is increasing in value.
  • Technical implementation work is evolving toward designing validation and automated evaluation mechanisms that accelerate iteration.
  • Higher ROI comes from integrating systems with telemetry and adaptive loops to provide more features and better meet customer needs, instead of squeezing the last fraction of a percent of model accuracy.
  • Three key high‑ROI focus areas:
  • • Precise problem definition.
  • • Robust evaluation and tuning mechanisms.
  • • Thoughtful cross‑domain combination.

Improving problem definition skills involves imagining what products could be more useful in real life, rather than being constrained by what current models can do.

Practical Advice for Developers

Problem definition: start from broad, real‑life usefulness instead of being constrained by existing components or current model capabilities.

Prompt and workflow design: micro‑level optimization work is shifting toward prompt strategy, workflow design, and multi‑modality integration.

Planner‑executor architecture: use a high‑level planner with specialized executors and feedback loops to limit error accumulation in large‑model applications.

Use LLMs as translators: treat an abstract or spec as a single source of truth that large language models can translate into slides, documents, diagrams, and code structure.

Career habits: make every step solid—ensure code works as well as it can, designs are good enough, and questions are pursued until fully understood.

Staying current: track AI research by skimming recommended papers for directional trends and explore new AI tools in a task‑driven way when you have concrete needs.

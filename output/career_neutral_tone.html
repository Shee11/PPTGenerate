Rewind to 2011

The tech journey starts in 2011 at Microsoft in computer vision, working on the Face API and other perception models that turned images into structured signals software could understand and act on.

Early career focused on computer vision and Face API, turning raw pixels into usable data for applications and services.

  • Domain: computer vision and perception models
  • Typical work: building and improving Face API
  • Why it mattered: brought recognition capabilities to apps
  • Industry shift: APIs started to package ML for developers
  • Lesson: strong fundamentals in a focused domain compound over time

The career path moves from traditional machine learning and computer vision into deep learning, then into cloud and distributed systems, and finally into large language models and AI agents.

Deep learning shift

Work moves from traditional approaches into deep learning and neural networks, using new architectures to push model accuracy and unlock capabilities that earlier methods could not reach.

Industry momentum builds around neural networks as data and compute grow, and teams start to retool pipelines and infrastructure around these models.

Key lesson: follow emerging architectures early, learn the underlying ideas, and be ready to rebuild skills as the modeling toolkit changes.

Mid-career work transitions from model-centric projects into cloud services, distributed systems, and scalability as AI features move into production.

  • Domain: cloud services and distributed systems
  • Focus: scalability, reliability, and system design
  • Why it mattered: models needed robust platforms to reach users
  • Industry shift: AI became a cloud-first, service-oriented capability
  • Lesson: understanding systems and scalability multiplies model impact
